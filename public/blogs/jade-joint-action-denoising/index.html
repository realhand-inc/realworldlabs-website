JADE: Bringing Vision-Language Models into the Action Loop

November 18, 2026Realworld Labs

Our research on joint action denoising brings the vision-language model into every sampling step, improving manipulation across simulation benchmarks and four real-world bimanual tasks.

A robot policy must connect what it sees and what it is asked to do with a precise sequence of physical actions. Our research explores how a pretrained vision-language model can stay involved throughout that sequence's generation.

We introduce JADE, the Joint Action DEnoiser: an extension of π0.5 that lets the vision-language model read the current action estimate and update its guidance at every denoising step. Across two simulation benchmarks and four real-world bimanual tasks, this design improves manipulation success over the base policy.

Inside JADE

Bring the VLM into the action loop.

In many diffusion- and flow-based vision-language-action models, the VLM first encodes images, an instruction, and the robot state. An action expert then iteratively refines a noisy action sequence using those fixed observation features.

JADE makes that guidance depend on the action being generated. At each denoising step, the VLM receives a compact representation of the current noisy action alongside the observation context. Its updated features help the action expert decide how to refine the sequence next.

A compact interface for continuous actions.

A pretrained, frozen Ordered Action Tokenization (OAT) encoder compresses a 50-step action chunk into eight four-dimensional continuous latent representations. A trainable adapter maps these representations into the VLM's input space. This gives the VLM a small, structured view of the evolving action.

Dynamic guidance, layer by layer.

The VLM processes these action representations with the observation context. Its layer-wise features are injected into the action expert through gated cross-attention. The gates regulate the additional guidance, while the expert continues to predict updates in continuous action space.

Observation-prefix keys and values are cached once per policy query. The compact action representations and their VLM features are recomputed at each denoising step. These are internal sampling steps for an action chunk; they do not require a new camera observation at every step.

Learn directly from clean actions.

We jointly fine-tune the VLM and action expert on demonstrations. Alongside the native flow-matching objective, an auxiliary alignment loss trains the VLM to recover clean action latents from noisy ones. This gives the VLM a direct action-learning signal. The OAT encoder stays frozen, and the alignment prediction head is used only during training.

Ablations support the contribution of both components: processing the current action with the VLM and injecting its features throughout the expert. Fixed queries, reused initial features, and direct OAT-to-expert controls perform below full JADE under the evaluated protocols.

Evaluation

Improvements in simulation and on real robots.

We evaluate JADE against its π0.5 base on RoboTwin 2.0, RoboDojo, and a physical dual-arm platform. The comparisons below use ten denoising steps for both policies.

RoboTwin 2.0 covers 50 bimanual tasks, with training on clean demonstrations and evaluation in both Clean and unseen Random scenes. JADE reaches 54.4% success in Random scenes, compared with 46.0% for the base policy.

RoboDojo covers 42 tasks across Generalization, Precision, Long-Horizon, Memory, and Open capabilities. The manuscript averages the five category means equally: JADE achieves a Score of 18.29 and a full-task success rate of 12.67%. Score and success rate are separate measures; the latter requires completing the task.

On the physical platform

Four tasks. Real-world motion.

Our real-world experiments use a PiPER dual-arm robot with one overhead camera and two wrist cameras. For each task, both policies are trained on the same 100 teleoperated demonstrations and evaluated over 30 trials. JADE improves success on all four tasks.

The deployment trade-off

Better guidance, fewer sampling steps.

Involving the VLM at every step increases the cost of each model evaluation. JADE's stronger few-step performance offers a way to balance that cost against task success.

Two-step JADE improves RoboDojo success over ten-step π0.5 at near-matched reported model latency. On RoboTwin, the same two-step configuration reaches 66.0% average success, compared with 57.3% for the ten-step base policy.

JADE Balanced adapts its sampling step size using the recent variation in predicted action corrections. It takes larger steps when the correction signal is stable and smaller steps when it fluctuates. On full RoboDojo evaluation, it uses 4.985 model evaluations per chunk on average and retains 98.5% of ten-step JADE's success rate.

These timings cover model computation only, excluding communication, simulation, and robot execution. Sampling configurations use the same trained checkpoint and keep the action-execution horizon unchanged. The best sampling schedule depends on the benchmark and the latency budget.

What comes next

A closer connection between perception and control.

JADE shows that action-conditioned VLM features can improve continuous action generation under the evaluated settings. Its central contribution is a practical interface through which the VLM and action expert can refine an evolving action together.

Important limitations remain. RoboDojo Open-category success is 2.42%, and some tasks still fail under scene changes or require more reliable multi-stage completion. The real-world evaluation uses 30 trials per task and policy; additional distribution-shift experiments are preliminary. These results support the tested design, without establishing general-purpose reasoning or robustness to arbitrary environments.

This work is presented in our research manuscript, Vision-Language Models Are Joint Denoisers in Robotic Action Generation. We are continuing to study how richer perception-action interaction can support more reliable embodied intelligence.
