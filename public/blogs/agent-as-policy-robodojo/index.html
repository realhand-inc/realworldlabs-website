Agent as Policy on RoboDojo: 42 Tasks, One General-Purpose Agent

October 16, 2026RealHand Inc.Originally published on RealHand

Our Agent as Policy evaluation spans 42 simulated dual-arm tasks and 2,100 episodes, reaching a 63.63 average score and 56.13% success rate on seed 0.

Putting an agent in the control loop.

What if the robot policy were a general-purpose AI agent? We evaluated Agent as Policy (AGP) on RoboDojo, where one agent observes, reasons, commands two robot arms, and checks the result across 42 manipulation tasks.

RoboDojo evaluates generalist policies on a simulated dual-arm ARX X5 robot. Randomized layouts test whether a policy can adapt its actions, handle precise contact, complete long sequences, remember earlier observations, and follow new instructions.

For RealHand, this is a way to study the intelligence behind manipulation: how a robot can turn an instruction into a sequence of physical actions, then use feedback to revise that sequence.

How AGP works

Observe. Reason. Act. Repeat.

AGP places a general-purpose LLM agent in charge of task execution. In this evaluation, GPT-6-Astra running in Codex uses camera observations and the robot's joint readings to decide what to do next. Motion planning translates the agent's targets into arm trajectories.

The same agent, prompt, and tools are used across all 42 tasks. AGP receives a task instruction and short written task notes, with no RoboDojo demonstrations, benchmark-specific training or fine-tuning, or access to hidden simulator state.

  1. Observe

    Read the overhead and two wrist-camera images.

  2. Measure

    Point to objects in the images to estimate where to act.

  3. Plan & move

    Choose arm targets; a motion planner generates trajectories.

  4. Verify & recover

    Look again, check progress, and correct slips or missed grasps.

  5. Finish

    Declare completion; RoboDojo's checker scores the episode.

Evaluation results

A strong result across five capabilities.

Our seed-0 run reaches 63.63 average score and 56.13% success. For context, EmbodiedEvo, the top entry in the October 14 leaderboard snapshot, records 45.08 and 38.95%. AGP scores higher than that entry in each of the five capability categories.

Evaluation scope

AGP: our own evaluation on seed 0, all 42 tasks and 2,100 episodes, scored with RoboDojo's unmodified official scoring script. The official leaderboard evaluates three seeds, so these comparisons use different seed coverage. An official three-seed evaluation may produce different results. Averages are the mean of the five category results.

What comes next

Toward more adaptable manipulation.

These simulation results give us a concrete way to test an agent's ability to reason through manipulation, use visual feedback, and recover when an action does not go to plan. They also highlight where reliability needs to improve, especially across long sequences. The linked project and datasets let researchers examine both the outcomes and the steps the agent took to reach them.
